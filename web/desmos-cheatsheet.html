The five that matter most

If you learn nothing else, learn these. They cover a large share of the calculator-section questions where Desmos beats algebra outright.

Type thisWhat it does
y=f(x)Graph anything and read the answer off the picture. Tap the curve to reveal zeros, intercepts and turning points — Desmos labels them for you.
y_{1}=…
y_{2}=…		Solving an equation? Graph each side separately and tap where they cross. The intersection is the solution. Works when factoring doesn't.
x=3A vertical line. Pair it with a curve to read a value off the graph instead of substituting by hand.
…{x>0}Restrict a graph to part of the domain. Useful for piecewise questions and "for positive x" wording.
y_{1}~ax_{1}+bLinear regression on a table. Gives you slope and intercept of the line of best fit in one line.

Typing shortcuts

Desmos rewrites what you type as proper math as you go. These are the keystrokes that get you there fastest.

PressYou get
/A fraction. Type the numerator, press /, type the denominator, press the right arrow to get out.
^An exponent. Right arrow leaves it — the single most common place students get stuck.
sqrtA square root sign. Typing the letters is enough; Desmos converts it.
_A subscript, for x_1 and y_1 in regressions.
piπ. Same idea — type the word.
absAbsolute value bars.

Question types worth a graph

QuestionFastest approach
System of equationsGraph both, tap the intersection. Faster than substitution almost every time, and immune to sign errors.
Quadratic rootsGraph it and tap the x-intercepts. No factoring, no formula.
Vertex or maximumGraph it and tap the turning point. Desmos labels the coordinates.
CirclesType the equation as given, even in unexpanded form. Desmos draws it and you read the centre and radius off the picture.
Line of best fitMake a table, then y_{1}~ax_{1}+b. Read a and b from the panel.
Exponential growthGraph it and tap the point you need. Beats rearranging logarithms under time pressure.
"For what value of x…"Graph both sides, or graph one side and add y= the target value. Tap the crossing.

When not to open Desmos

This is the part most guides skip. Reaching for the calculator has a cost — roughly ten to fifteen seconds of typing — and on some questions that's the whole time you had.

  • Arithmetic you can do in your head. Just do it in your head.
  • Sum or product of roots — −b/a and c/a are faster than graphing, if you remember them.
  • Questions asking for an expression rather than a number. Graphs give numbers.
  • Anything where you already see the answer. Trust that.
